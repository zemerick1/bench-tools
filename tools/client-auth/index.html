    Glossary

    The words this tab uses, once. Nothing here changes the cartoon.

    EAP, RADIUS, NAC, and the rest
    EAP
    Extensible Authentication Protocol. What the device speaks to the authenticator. Left wire. Not RADIUS.
    NAC / authentication server
    The box on the right that decides if you get on — ClearPass, ISE, the usual suspects. People call it “the RADIUS server.” RADIUS is the protocol that box speaks with the AP, not the name of the box.
    RADIUS
    A protocol, not a hostname. Access-Request, Access-Challenge, Access-Accept, Access-Reject. It runs on the right wire, between the authenticator and the NAC. The device never speaks it.
    Authenticator
    The AP or switch in the middle. In RADIUS this box is the client (NAS). The laptop is not a RADIUS client.
    PEAP
    Protected EAP. Server cert, then a password inside a TLS tunnel. One cert, a secret in a trench coat.
    EAP-TLS
    Both sides brought a cert. No password. Mutual trust or nobody gets on.
    TTLS / EAP-TTLS
    Tunneled TLS. Server cert outside, PAP or MSCHAPv2 or another TLS inside. The AP still does not see the inner password.
    TEAP
    Two methods in a trench coat. Inner methods can chain. Still two wires.
    MAC auth
    The authenticator sent the MAC address to the NAC over RADIUS. Nobody spoke EAP. If it works, it is not 802.1X.
    OCSP
    Online Certificate Status Protocol. Ask the signer if this cert is still good. Skip means nobody asked. Revoked means the answer was no.
    Access-Accept
    A RADIUS packet: the NAC said yes. The right wire’s happy ending. Not an EAP-Success by itself, though they usually travel as a pair in this cartoon.

    FAQ

    Does the device ever talk to the NAC?

    No. Left wire is EAP to the AP or switch. Right wire is RADIUS — the protocol — to the NAC. The device does not have the NAC IP. People say “RADIUS server” the way they say “Kleenex” for tissue. If a hop ever drew device ↔ NAC, this tab would be lying.

    What do the cert knobs actually check?

    Signer, expiry, and OCSP. Not a full PKI lab. Unknown signer, expired, wrong name, or revoked — any of those fail closed. The cartoon stops. It does not keep bouncing.

    Is MAC auth EAP?

    No. Nobody spoke EAP. The authenticator sent a MAC to the NAC over RADIUS and hoped. History will show zero EAP hops. That is the lesson.

    Does the AP learn the password?

    No. Inner credentials ride inside the tunnel. The AP forwards EAP. It does not get a copy of the password, and it cannot “see PEAP.” That is why the inner knobs only appear for tunneled methods.
