Subnet Planner

If everything is on VLAN 1 today, this is the from-scratch plan for separating infrastructure, users, IoT, and the rest — by building, with headroom and room to grow. It is not a CIDR calculator with a marketing team.

What this is: a greenfield IPv4 plan. You say how many buildings and how many devices per role at each building. We pick masks (you may oversize), assign VLANs if you shrug, mesh the blocks so they don’t overlap, and leave spacing so tomorrow’s growth doesn’t renumber yesterday’s neighbor.

What this is not: brownfield IPAM, DHCP config, floor/room design, or IPv6 (still a myth here). Quiet output does not mean your production network is fine — this tab has never met your production network.
Stays in this tab. No upload, no cloud “design AI,” no surprise bill. Refresh and the plan evaporates with your dignity.
Plan for growth. Every size includes at least 50% headroom on the count you enter. Subnets get a reserved lane (one bit wider) so packing them back-to-back doesn’t trap you. If you already know you’ll need a cathedral, oversize the prefix — we allow greed, not undersizing.

1. Buildings

One row per building. Floors and rooms are out of scope. Site number is human numbering: site 1 is addressed as slot 1 (e.g. 10.10.1.0/…), not zero. Leave it blank and we assign 1, 2, 3… in row order.

2. Do buildings need to talk to each other?

Example: can a PC at HQ reach a camera or printer at another building over your network (VPN, SD-WAN, or similar)?

3. Roles (device types / VLANs)

Name the purpose. Enter how many devices you expect per building for that role (e.g. Students — 1000). VLAN ID optional (we assign 10, 20, 30…). Prefix override optional — only to go bigger than the math (never smaller than /25). Infrastructure is pre-loaded because switches and APs deserve their own quiet VLAN.

4. Private address space

Greenfield only. We assume you get the whole block for this design. 10.x is the grown-up default.

IPv6 is not invited.

5. Site number: second or third number in the IP?

In an address like 10.a.b.c, a is the second number and b is the third. Pick where site goes. Site numbers are human 1, 2, 3… (site 1 is never “computer zero”).

6. Generate the plan

We mesh roles into aligned parents, slice spaced lanes per building, put the live subnet at the start of each lane, gateway .1. Validation will refuse undersize, VLAN 1 cosplay, duplicate VLANs, and empty names. Oversizing is a free country.

FAQ

Is this a subnet calculator?

A calculator tells you the broadcast of a prefix you already chose. This builds the scheme for you — the table you use when designing (or redesigning) addressing before vlan 1 becomes “the plan” at every building.

Why isn’t everything a /24?

Because 1000 students + 50% headroom is not 254 addresses. We size from device counts. Small roles still get at least a /25 — we do not hand out /27 or /28 scraps. You may oversize.

What’s a “reserved lane” / “reservation ends”?

The live subnet is what you use today. The reserved lane is extra room so that site can grow without taking the next building’s addresses. Reservation ends is the last IP still held for that building/role — stay at or below it when you expand.

Can I add another role later (Cameras, Voice, …)?

Yes. Each role gets its own second-octet block under 10.<vlan>.… (or the private space you chose). Auto VLAN IDs step 10, 20, 30… so unused IDs in between are free if you set them by hand. After you generate a plan, the Room to grow box shows the next free auto VLAN and how many second-octet slots are still open. Adding a role means re-running the planner with that row included (greenfield redesign is cheap; renumbering production is not).

Do buildings need to talk to each other?

If devices at one building should reach devices at another over the network, choose Yes so every subnet is unique. If buildings are completely separate forever, choose No — we still use unique addresses in this greenfield plan.

Brownfield?

Not this tool. This is “if I designed it from scratch.” Existing 10.10.0.0/16 landmines are your archaeology project.
